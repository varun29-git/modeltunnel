# Terminus Lean H100 real-token throughput, 2026-10-01

This receipt covers a bounded throughput check of the existing 146,423,680-parameter dense-FP8 Terminus Lean model. It is not a model-quality experiment or a completed 3B-token run.

## Setup

- Source commit: `a24b29358435cf54b41c89c28cb2f3a5ba2fac69`.
- Model: `experiments/terminus_lean_150m_3b/configs/lean_dense.json`; 25 layers, width 640, 4,096-token context, 512-token local attention window, one global layer per five layers. Dense FP8 weight and activation operands, full FP8 backward, DeepGEMM linears, FlashAttention-3, Triton loss, Muon optimizer, BF16 residuals.
- Hardware: one Azure `Standard_NCC40ads_H100_v5` H100 NVL (99,456,909,312 usable GPU-memory bytes), PyTorch 2.8.0+cu126, Triton 3.4.0. CUDA 13.2 was used for native JIT compilation.
- Frozen tokenizer SHA-256: `c53e0e95cd19e2954a8c7114c0a580eeb16698b153fdf84c5d80a7187d778d29`.
- Native GPU preflight passed before training. Preflight JSON SHA-256: `bb477c290dca63fe59d271d4df1db30afba0baf67731d3dd3d0c269a3cc0a4b6`.
- Training data consisted of completed, shuffled, pretokenized train and held-out validation shards from the active AWS tokenization job. The bounded benchmark scope was English 50%, code 10%, math 10%, and Hindi, Gujarati, Bengali, Kannada 7.5% each. Each source had a train and validation shard. The remaining three campaign Indic streams were still being tokenized and were not included. The benchmark manifest had a 16,777,216-token budget and passed production manifest validation.
- The model and production kernels were unchanged. For this short check, the WSD schedule used 2,097,152 warmup tokens and a 4,096-token decay, and routine checkpoint/validation intervals were moved beyond the 12 measured updates. The stop checkpoint and validation still completed. Stability probes remained enabled at their configured interval.

## Results

The table reports the median of logged token rates from updates 2–9. Those eight updates exclude initialization and the configured stability-sampling updates. Each update includes token loading, forward and backward passes, clipping, and Muon. The rate excludes final checkpoint writing and held-out validation.

| Microbatch | Accumulation | Tokens/update | Real-token median tokens/s | Peak reserved GPU bytes |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 32 | 2 | 262,144 | 243,645 | 63,466,110,976 |
| 48 | 2 | 393,216 | 250,956 | 94,168,416,256 |
| 50 | 2 | 409,600 | 252,164 | 98,098,479,104 |

All three real-token runs completed 12 updates, their final checkpoint and held-out validation, and recorded no incidents. A separate synthetic fitting sweep found microbatch 51, 52, and 64 out of memory; microbatch 50 was the largest tested that fit. The synthetic rates are not used as the real-token result.

Microbatch 50 delivered the highest observed rate, about 0.5% above 48, but left only 1.36 GB of reported GPU memory headroom. Microbatch 48 left 5.29 GB. For a longer run, 48 is the better starting point until checkpoint, validation, and longer-duration stability are checked at 50. The measured update rate implies roughly 3.3 H100-hours for 3B tokens of updates alone; data preparation, startup, validation, checkpointing, and any long-run slowdowns add time.

The Azure benchmark artifacts remain under `/data/work/terminus_bench_20261001/` on the deallocated VM. No token shards or checkpoints were committed. The temporary S3 transfer object was removed after staging.
